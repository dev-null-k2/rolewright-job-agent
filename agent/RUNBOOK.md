# Rolewright runbook (followed by the scheduled job scan)

Tracker page: the Rolewright artifact URL in `profile.json` (`trackerUrl`). Database: collections `jobs`, `contacts`, `posts`, `hiringPosts`, `network` and docs `meta/scan`, `meta/inbox`, `meta/resume`, `meta/profile`, `meta/answers`, `meta/weekly`.
All files below live in the Claude Project folder `rolewright/` (read them with project_read).
Scanner script: `rolewright/scan_jobs.py`. Role types: AI/agents/FDE/applied AI first, plus software, backend, Python, full stack, data engineering, platform/SRE and ML roles. Each role also gets salary (when the posting shows pay), type (full time / contract / contract-to-hire), area (the areas in profile.json, or Remote) and h1bFilings (the employer's past H-1B LCA filings from US Department of Labor data, a sign it hires international candidates). Sources: about 180 Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby boards (AI labs, AI startups and tech), 23 Workday career sites (NVIDIA, Salesforce, Adobe, Workday, Mastercard, BlackRock, Citi, Bank of America, Intel, PayPal, S&P Global, Pfizer, Morgan Stanley, Capital One, AT&T, Fidelity, McKesson, Sabre, USAA, State Street, TIAA, Verizon, Travelers), Amazon, Microsoft, Google careers (the cities in profile.json), the current Hacker News 'Who is hiring?' thread (often with founder emails in `contact`), Dice (the profile's locations and remote; this is where most staffing and contract firms post: Robert Half, Kforce, Insight Global, Apex, Motion Recruitment, Jobot, CyberCoders, Randstad, NTT Data and consulting vendors; ats "dice"), the TEKsystems, Actalent and Aston Carter career sites (ats "phenom"), JPMorgan Chase and Oracle (ats "oracle"), and Apple (ats "apple"). LinkedIn, Indeed and Glassdoor come in through their job-alert emails in Gmail (step 2b).

## Candidate (use only these facts in drafts; never invent)
Read `rolewright/profile.md` (who the candidate is, their strongest results, targets, what never to mention) and `rolewright/RESUME_MASTER.md` (their current resume, the only source of truth for facts and numbers). If the two disagree, the resume wins. `rolewright/profile.json` holds the tracker URL, name, portfolio link, search areas and the words never to mention.

## Daily steps
1. Read `rolewright/scan_jobs.py`, `rolewright/find_contacts.py`, `rolewright/linkedin_posts.py`, `rolewright/check_drafts.py`, `rolewright/h1b_sponsors.json`, `rolewright/profile.json` and `rolewright/RESUME_MASTER.md` from the project (project_read; large docs come back as a local file path: copy that file), save them in one folder, and run `python3 scan_jobs.py --days 2 --out jobs.json --sponsors h1b_sponsors.json --profile profile.json`.
2b. Gmail job alerts: search Gmail (Gmail connector) with `from:(jobalerts-noreply@linkedin.com OR jobs-noreply@linkedin.com OR alert@indeed.com OR noreply@glassdoor.com) newer_than:2d`, read each thread (get_thread, PLAIN_TEXT), and pull out each job (title, company, location, link). Keep ones that fit the targets below; give them ids like `li-<company>-<short title slug>` and treat them like scanner results (ats "linkedin-alert" etc.). Never open links that need a login.
2. List the tracker's `jobs` collection (ArtifactData list, limit 1000) to get existing ids. Skip any job already present.
3. Keep new roles with fit >= 1 (3/2 = AI, agents, FDE, applied AI; 1 = software, backend, Python, full stack, data engineering, platform/SRE, ML), visa != "no-sponsorship"; drop roles requiring security clearance or located only outside the profile's areas and US remote. Skip "Lead"/"Staff"/"Principal"/manager titles. Google results have location from the search, not the posting: keep them only if the title fits. Hacker News posts: keep ones in the profile's areas or US remote that mention AI/LLM/agents in the role; put any email from `contact` in a `contactEmail` field and say in `why` that the post lists a direct contact. No daily cap: add every new role that passes these filters (expect 400–600 a day), best fit and newest first; skip roles asking for 8+ years. For Dice/staffing roles (ats "dice" or "phenom"): prefer full time and W2 contract over contractor posts from small unnamed vendors; skip anything that says C2C/corp-to-corp only, 1099 only, or USC/GC only (the scanner flags most of these as no-sponsorship); in `why` say it is a staffing/contract role and who posted it; contactSearch should look for the recruiter at the posting firm (https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=<firm>%20technical%20recruiter%20<city>).
4. For each kept role write a doc (copy these scanner fields as they are: jd (the scanner `summary`, first 900 characters),  salary, salaryMin, type, area, h1bFilings, h1bLastFY; LinkedIn/Indeed alert roles: fill area from the location, type "full time" unless it says contract, salary if the email shows one, h1bFilings 0) (build the docs in a local JSON file per 50 roles and write each with one ArtifactData batch using file_path, so hundreds of roles go in quickly; if the run is getting long, finish the writes first and keep drafts to 40–50 words) in `jobs` (doc_id = the scanner id) with: company, title, location, url, posted, visa, fit, status "new", found (today), why (one line on why it fits), companyName (the company as people write it: "Capital One", "Ait Global", "NTT Data"), subject (email subject, under 60 characters, role in plain words plus the candidate's name, e.g. "Data Engineer role – <Name>"; staffing: "Your AI Engineer posting – <Name>"), draft (follow the Draft rules section below exactly) and draftV 4, resumeTip (one line: which 2 resume points to put first for this posting), contactSearch (LinkedIn people-search URL for the likely hiring manager: https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=<company>%20<team>%20manager). Contacts: when the scanner result has contactEmail and/or contactName, copy contactEmail, contactName, contactTitle, contactKind, contactSource, contactProfile and contactVerified exactly as given (and its contactSearch if present, instead of building one). These come only from the posting itself and every email's domain was checked for a mail server; never edit them, never guess an address or a name, and never put a person's name in contactName unless the scanner gave it. A role with no scanner contact keeps contactName/contactEmail empty (the page then offers the LinkedIn search).
4c. Contact finder (every run): dump `jobs` (status new) with out_dir, run `python3 find_contacts.py <dump>/jobs contacts_out`, and write each file in contacts_out as an ArtifactData update (batch, 50 per call, if_version from the listing). It re-reads Dice postings for roles still without a contact and only returns published addresses whose domain has a mail server. Never add contacts any other way except the Gmail step below.
5. Update `meta/scan` with lastScan (today, in the candidate's time zone), lastScanAt (the exact UTC time the run finished, from `date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ`, taken right before this write), boards (count), newToday, total (jobs count after the run).
5b. Housekeeping (keeps the tracker under its 25,000-document limit): if the jobs collection has more than 15,000 docs, delete docs with status "new" or "skipped" whose `found` is older than 21 days (batch delete with if_version, 50 at a time) until it is under 12,000. Never delete jobs with any other status, and never touch `contacts` or `posts`.
6. Mondays only (once per day): add one LinkedIn post draft to `posts` (doc_id = date; fields date, topic, status "draft", text 120–200 words) about one concrete lesson from the candidate's own work, using only facts from the resume and profile. Rotate topics listed in profile.md, if any.
7. Read `contacts`; count those with followUpOn <= today and status not replied/closed.
8. Finish with a short summary: new roles added (top 5 with company and title), follow-ups due, and the tracker link.

## Draft rules (outreach is sent as the candidate; it has to make a busy person want to reply)
- Never mention where the candidate lives, relocation, visa or work authorization in any draft, subject, referral ask, cover letter or reply. The words in profile.json `neverMention` are blocked by the checker.
The bar: a hiring manager reads it in 10 seconds and thinks "this person has already done this job". Safe, polite and generic is a failure.
- Open with the strongest claim that maps to THEIR role, not with "I applied" or "I'm a software engineer". profile.md lists the candidate's best claim for each role type; use the one that matches.
- Give the result as business impact, biggest first (time or money saved, scale, adoption). Pick the one or two numbers that matter most to that team. Every number must appear in RESUME_MASTER.md.
- One clause on why THIS team: the customers, product or problem named in the posting `summary`. If it gives nothing specific, use the team or office named in the title. Never fake familiarity.
- Close with a confident, specific ask: "Worth 15 minutes this week?" / "Can we talk Thursday or Friday?". Direct-employer roles may end with the profile's `portfolioLink` on its own.
- 55–75 words. Short sentences, contractions, no semicolons, no em dashes. Starts "Hi [Name],".
- Never: "I applied for", "reaching out", "quick note", "I saw", "caught my eye", "I'd love to", "excited", "passionate", "happy to", "leverage", "align", "Is the role still open?", "Hi there". No internal jargon a recruiter wouldn't know.
- Say roles the way a person would ("the Forward Deployed Engineer role on your life sciences team", never "(FDE), Life Sciences - NYC"). Company names as people write them.
- Full-time roles posted on Dice by the employer itself: write like a direct-employer draft; never ask for "client, duration and rate".
- Staffing (dice, phenom): to the recruiter. Lead with the fit in one line, give the stack match and one number, say the candidate is available, ask for client, duration and rate. Never offer or mention the resume: the tracker adds "I've attached my resume for your reference." and the signature to every email. No portfolio link.
- Honesty check on fit: if the posting's core requirement is something the candidate has not done (per the resume), do not claim it "matches" or is a "direct fit": skip clear mismatches, or write honestly about the overlap.
- Staffing drafts must not be copies of each other: vary the opener and pick the stack items that appear in that posting.
- Subjects: the role title in plain words, then " – <candidate name>". Never "Your ... posting", no city, brackets, codes, req numbers, "W2" or "Only locals". The tracker cleans posting titles the same way when it builds the email.
- No greeting beyond "Hi [Name]," and no sign-off: the tracker adds them.
- Same company, several roles: ONE full draft on the best-fit role (mention in a few words that they'd also fit the other open roles); the others get empty draft + sameAs. Never put notes inside `draft`.
- Example shapes (vary wording, never copy): FDE: "Hi [Name], I've spent the past year doing the forward deployed job without the title. <where and what you shipped, with the people who use it>. <biggest result>. I want to do that for <company>'s customers. Worth 15 minutes this week?" Staffing: "Hi [Name], your <role> contract fits what I do: <stack items from the posting> in production, <one number>. I'm available now. What's the client, duration and rate?"
- Top roles (fit 3, direct employer): open the posting URL and read it fully before writing; the draft must name one concrete thing from it. The checker fails fit-3 drafts that share no distinctive word with the posting.
- Before writing, score every draft 1–5 on "would a hiring manager reply?". Rewrite anything under 4.

## Contact lookup step (every run, after step 4)
For up to 60 new roles per run with fit >= 2 from direct employers (best fit first), search Gmail read-only for people at that company who have emailed the candidate: `"<companyName>" -from:me newer_than:365d` and, when the company's email domain is obvious, `from:@<domain>`. If a real person at that company wrote to them (a recruiter, hiring manager or interviewer; not a no-reply or marketing address), set contactName and contactEmail on the job (update with if_version). The page then fills the To line, subject and greeting in one click. Never send anything.

## Redraft step (every run, after step 4)
First fix legacy notes: any status-"new" doc whose draft starts with "Same company as" gets the proper treatment (empty draft + sameAs pointing to the best role at that company), and make sure that best role has a full draft and no sameAs.
List `jobs` docs with status "new" that do not have draftV 4. Rewrite their `draft` (and `resumeTip` if missing) under the Draft rules, highest fit first, all of them every run (work in batches of 50; if the run is very long, finish the highest-fit ones first), using title, company, why and the scanner summary when the id is in today's jobs.json. Write with update + if_version, setting draft (or empty draft + sameAs), draftV 4, companyName and subject (and resumeTip if missing). If a role is clearly not a fit (AI trainer, data annotation/labeling, tutoring, rater work, needs a license the candidate doesn't have), set its status to "skipped" instead. Do not touch any other field or any doc whose status is not "new".

## Interview prep step (every run)
For each job whose status is "interviewing" and that has no `prep` field: read the posting (url) and the company's site, then write `prep` (plain text, under 350 words): what the company and team do; the 5 questions most likely for this role; for each, which of the candidate's stories to use (only facts from the resume and profile); 3 smart questions to ask them; and anything to check first (salary range from the posting, location/onsite). Update with if_version. Also, for contacts with status "scheduled", put the same kind of prep in the contact's `message` if it has none.

## Follow-up step (every run)
For jobs with status "messaged" or "applied" whose `followUp` date is today or earlier and that have no `followUpDraft`: write `followUpDraft` (25–40 words, polite nudge, same Draft rules, references the first note in one line, no guilt-trip). The page's Today tab lists due follow-ups.

## Hard check step (every run, after any draft is written; mandatory)
Use `check_drafts.py`, `profile.json` and `RESUME_MASTER.md` saved next to the scanner in step 1 (the checker also verifies every `resumeVersion` against the master: no new numbers, tools or names). List `jobs` with ArtifactData using out_dir (e.g. out_dir=./db), and `hiringPosts` the same way, then run `python3 check_drafts.py ./db/jobs --posts ./db/hiringPosts --json fails.json` (it also checks coverLetter, whyCompany, contacts and hiring-post messages). Every listed failure must be fixed: rewrite that draft (and subject) so it passes, write it back with if_version, re-list and re-run the checker. Repeat until it prints "0 of N drafts fail" (at most 4 rounds). If a draft still fails after 4 rounds, set its draft to "" and draftV to 0 so the next run starts it fresh. The page also runs these same checks and will not offer a failing draft for sending, so a failing draft costs a role its outreach until fixed. Never quote numbers from the posting itself (team size, customers, funding): only the candidate's facts. Report the final checker line in the summary.

## Draft audit step (every run, last)
Review every status-"new" draft written in the last 2 days against the Draft rules, especially the honesty check on fit, staffing variety and subjects. Fix or skip offenders (update with if_version). Report how many you fixed.

## Match score step (every run, for new roles)
Give every new role `score` (1–5, how likely the candidate gets an interview: skills overlap with the posting, seniority, location, sponsorship signals, company type) and `scoreWhy` (one plain line, e.g. "Python + LLM agents match; asks 5+ yrs, has 3; onsite"). Be strict: 5 means near-perfect fit. The page's "Best match first" sort and Today list use it.

## Learn-from-skips step (every run, before step 3)
List jobs with status "skipped" and a `skipReason`. Summarise the patterns from the last 60 (companies, title words, locations, pay, seniority the candidate keeps skipping) and apply them as extra filters and score penalties for today's new roles. Mention in the summary what you learned.

## Closed-posting check (every run)
For new roles with score >= 4 (and any role whose draft you are about to write for a fit-3 direct employer), open the posting URL. If it returns 404/410, redirects to a generic careers page, or says the job is closed / no longer accepting applications / filled, set `closed` true and `closedAt` (today) and do not draft it. Also re-check roles marked "new" that are older than 14 days, 50 per run.

## Referral step (every run)
Read the `network` collection (the candidate's LinkedIn connections: rows of n=name, c=company, p=position, u=profile URL). For each new role with score >= 3 at a company where they have a connection (match company names loosely: ignore Inc/LLC/Corp), set `referralTo` (name and position), `referralUrl` (their profile URL) and `referralDraft`: 40–60 words to that person, warm and direct, mentions the specific role and link, one line on why they fit, asks if they'd be open to a referral or pointing them to the hiring manager. Same banned-phrase rules as drafts; never mention visa.

## Tailored resume step (every run, top roles only)
Read `rolewright/RESUME_MASTER.md` (the current resume, the only source of truth). For the 10 highest-scoring new roles from direct employers (and every role marked "applied" or "interviewing" that has none), write `resumeVersion` in the same markdown shape as the master (## section headings, ### job lines, "- " bullets; no name/contact header, the page adds it). Allowed changes:
- Title line under the name: pick the closest honest one for the role.
- Summary: about as long as the master's, aimed at the role, using the posting's own words for things the candidate has really done.
- Bullets: reorder so the 3 most relevant come first. Keep every bullet from the master: the page lays the tailored resume out in exactly the original's format (same font, sizes and spacing), so it must stay about as long as the master and fill the page the same way. Drop a bullet only if the checker says the version runs past one page. You may reword a bullet to use the posting's term for the same thing, keeping its length about the same.
- Job, project and education lines (### lines and Education lines) are copied exactly from the master, including client and location. Never change or drop them.
- Skills: reorder so the posting's matching skills come first; remove irrelevant ones.
Never: add a skill, tool, employer, title, date, metric or claim that is not in RESUME_MASTER.md; change any number; inflate scope. Add `resumeChanges` (one line listing what was reordered/reworded) so the candidate can see the edits. If RESUME_MASTER.md is missing, skip this step and say so. The page shows a word-by-word diff of every tailored resume against the master (stored in the tracker at meta/resume) and blocks any version that adds a number or name not in the master, changes a job line, runs past one page, or is much shorter than the original. check_drafts.py runs the same checks (it lays the resume out exactly like the PDF), so fix every resume failure it lists.

## Cover letter step (every run, same roles as the tailored resume step, plus the top 30 new direct-employer roles by score for whyCompany)
For each role that gets a tailored resume (and every role marked "applied" or "interviewing" without one), write `coverLetter`: 150–220 words, plain text, starting exactly "Dear Hiring Team," and ending with the line "<the candidate's full name from profile.json>". Three short paragraphs: (1) what this team is building and the one detail from the posting that matches their work, (2) the two results from RESUME_MASTER.md that matter most for this role, in plain words, (3) a direct close asking for a conversation. Use only facts and numbers from RESUME_MASTER.md and profile.md; never mention visa, work authorization, location, relocation or anything in profile.json `neverClaim`; none of the banned phrases from the Draft rules; no em dashes or semicolons.
For the top 30 new direct-employer roles by score that have no `whyCompany`, write `whyCompany`: 30–70 words in first person answering "Why do you want to work here?" for that company and role, naming something specific about the company's product or team (from the posting or its site) and tying it to one real result. Same fact rules.
Update with if_version. The page shows both with copy and PDF buttons and holds back any that fail check_drafts.py.

## LinkedIn hiring posts step (every run)
LinkedIn has no public post-search API and its terms forbid scraping, so never log in to or fetch linkedin.com pages and never automate anything on LinkedIn. Instead use the WebSearch tool (mode "extended") for public posts, for example: `linkedin.com/posts hiring "forward deployed engineer"`, `linkedin.com/posts "we're hiring" "AI engineer" <each profile city>`, `linkedin.com/posts hiring "applied AI" <each profile city>`, `linkedin.com/posts hiring "AI engineer" remote US`, `linkedin.com/posts hiring "LLM" engineer`, `linkedin.com/posts hiring "agentic" engineer`, `linkedin.com/posts hiring "data engineer" <your cities> OR remote`, `linkedin.com/posts "#hiring" "machine learning engineer"`, `linkedin.com/posts "I'm hiring" engineer AI` (add allowed_domains ["linkedin.com"]). Save every result as {title, url, snippet} in results.json, list the `hiringPosts` collection ids into ids.txt, and run `python3 linkedin_posts.py results.json --days 14 --existing ids.txt > candidates.json` (it dates each post from its activity id and drops old, duplicate and non-hiring ones; candidates.json has "posts" and "people"). Search results are often profiles and job pages rather than posts, so expect few posts; that is fine, never pad the list. Keep only "posts" candidates that are a real person or company hiring for target roles (AI, FDE, applied AI, software, backend, data, ML) in the US target areas or US remote; drop job-board spam, India/EU-only posts, training/annotation gigs and anything older than 14 days. For each kept post write a doc in `hiringPosts` (doc_id = the candidate id) with: url, postedAt, poster (the person's name if the title or snippet gives it, else ""), company, role, area, text (the snippet), status "new", found (today), and draft: a LinkedIn message to the poster following the Draft rules (35–90 words, starts "Hi [Name],", one detail from their post, the strongest matching result, ends with a question; no subject). Run check_drafts.py with `--posts <dump of hiringPosts>` in the Hard check step too.

## People-to-try step (every run, for direct employers without a published contact)
Big employers (Google, OpenAI, Capital One and the like) do not publish recruiter emails. For the 25 direct-employer companies with the best-scoring new roles that have no contactEmail and no `linkedinPeople` yet, run WebSearch (mode "extended", allowed_domains ["linkedin.com"]) with `linkedin.com/in "<company>" (recruiter OR "talent acquisition" OR "engineering manager") AI` and, for FDE roles, `linkedin.com/in "<company>" "forward deployed"`. Feed all results through `python3 linkedin_posts.py results.json` and use its "people" list. Keep at most 3 people whose headline names that company and a recruiting, talent, hiring-manager, engineering-manager, head/lead/director or founder role. Write them on every new role at that company as `linkedinPeople`: [{name, headline, url}] (update with if_version). These are public search results for the candidate to look at and message themselves on LinkedIn; never put them in contactName or contactEmail, never guess an email from a name, and never visit or automate LinkedIn.
